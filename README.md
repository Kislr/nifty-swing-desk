# Nifty Swing Desk

A self-hosted, rules-based swing-trading desk for NSE stocks. Every weekday after the close, a scheduled job pulls the NSE market-watch table, stores a snapshot in this repository, recomputes sector leadership and trade setups, and sends you a Telegram digest. A static website (GitHub Pages) shows the full picture: regime, the momentum portfolio, sector board, swing setups (watch-only since 5 Nov 2026 — see "Where things stand"), the universe table, and a trade journal scored in R-multiples.

Everything runs on free tiers: GitHub (hosting, scheduler, storage) and Telegram (alerts). There is no server to maintain and no database account to create.

**This is a decision aid built on fixed rules, not investment advice.** Nothing it prints is a recommendation to buy or sell.

**Decisions taken (29 Oct 2026, before any backtest result existed)**

- *Deciles on a short list → rank the whole screenable universe.* With 20–45 passers a decile is 2–4 stocks and one takeover or blow-up decides a cohort, so the 70% test would be reading noise. The pass/fail test therefore ranks every screenable company (~3,100) on the composite score and compares top and bottom deciles of ~300, which also asks the right question: does the score separate winners from losers across all filers, not only among names already chosen. Sanity check for free: the quality list should sit inside the top decile. The composite must handle failing or missing traits (a failed trait scores, it is not "not applicable"). Passers-only terciles stay as a second, descriptive table, since that is the list one would actually buy.
- *Delisted companies → measure the gap and tag it from EDGAR, don't guess.* Yahoo has prices only for stocks still listed, so every past cohort is survivors only. Picks of a quality screen tend to leave by takeover (flatters the screen); rejects tend to leave by failure (also flatters it). No free source fixes this, so the backtest must report, for every cohort and every cap bucket, the share of the universe with no price history, and tag each missing name from the EDGAR store as *stopped filing* (with the date of the last 10-K) or *still filing* (a ticker change Yahoo mislabelled). Results carry a "survivor gap: X%" line until a delisted-price source exists.
- *If the gap is large → raise the cap floor, don't drop small caps on principle.* Delistings cluster at the bottom of the band, so the lever is the $300M floor in `screen.json`: set it at the lowest cap where the gap falls under about 5%, judged from the per-bucket measurement, not in advance. Reasons not to cut small caps wholesale: that is where quality and momentum effects are strongest and where a compounder screen is meant to look; mid-caps delist too, mostly by takeover, so a smaller gap is not automatically a more honest one; and if the floor climbs past about $2B the benchmark must change from the Russell 2000 to the S&P MidCap 400 or the comparison flatters the screen. If the gap is bad everywhere below $1B, the band becomes $1B–$10B and the report says so plainly.

## The momentum engine

A second engine with a different philosophy, kept separate so each is accountable to its own backtest. Every day it ranks the universe on its return over the last six months *excluding the most recent month* (short-term reversal is the best-documented wrinkle in momentum), drops anything thinly traded or more than 20% below its 52-week high, and lists the top 15, at most four per sector. The site shows this list in the **Momentum portfolio** card and the Telegram digest leads with it (since 5 Nov 2026; the swing setups follow as levels to watch).

The rules of use (both 13 Nov 2026 switches on): hold the list equal-weighted; rebalance every 21 trading days, selling anything that has fallen below rank 30 — or is no longer on the ranked list at all, because it is more than 20% under its high, thinly traded or out of the index (sold as "ineligible": about one exit in six) — and refilling from the top; a slot freed by a stop, or left empty by a closed gate, is filled at the next close the gate is open; new buys are half size while the Nifty's 20-day volatility is above its one-year median; between rebalances the only exit is a 20% fall from the highest close since entry. A **regime gate** — Nifty 50 above its 200-day average — decides whether new buys are allowed at all; when it is closed, holdings run out on their stops. Expect 35–50% winners: momentum earns from a few large winners and many small quick losses, so the win rate is the wrong number to watch.

node scripts/backtest-momentum.mjs --sweep               # 96-cell parameter map

## The compounder screen (shelved 5 Nov 2026)

**Shelved.** Both backtest attempts the rule in `compounder/SCREEN.md` allows failed — top decile over bottom decile in 57% and then 50% of 44 cohorts against a 70% bar — and the reasons, with what would reopen it, are in `reports/compounder-shelved-2026-11-05.md`. The store and the weekly report keep running; the quality list is a research list, not a signal. What follows describes what was built.

A fourth engine with a different horizon: a quality screen over fundamentals (return on capital, gross margin, growth without dilution, cash conversion, debt), with the momentum engine borrowed as its timing layer. The screen is written down in `compounder/screen.json` and explained in `compounder/SCREEN.md`, including the backtest's pass criteria, fixed before any data was pulled. US first; India later.

**Data (US).** `scripts/compounder-fetch-us.mjs` builds `compounder/data/us/universe.json` (every NYSE / Nasdaq filer with CIK, SIC, fiscal year end, flagged financial / 10-K filer) and `compounder/data/us/facts/NN.json` (per company, per fiscal-year end, the sixteen line items the five traits need). Both come from SEC EDGAR, free, with no account. The store is **point-in-time**: for each fiscal year it keeps the value from the earliest 10-K that reported it, with the filing date, and records any later restatement separately. That is what lets the backtest only use what was knowable on each date.

```
node scripts/compounder-fetch-us.mjs --universe                 # ~5,000 submissions lookups, ~10 min
node scripts/compounder-fetch-us.mjs --facts --limit 50         # quick test
npm run compounder:us                                           # everything, ~30 min
```

The job `.github/workflows/compounder-us.yml` runs this quarterly and commits the result; run it by hand from the Actions tab the first time. The SEC asks that pulls identify themselves: set a repository variable `EDGAR_USER_AGENT` (Settings → Secrets and variables → Actions → Variables) such as `nifty-swing-desk you@example.com`. Requests stay under the SEC's 10-per-second limit.

**The screen.** `compounder.js` evaluates the five traits for every company as of a date, using only filings made on or before it, and ranks the passers inside the market-cap band by rank-sum across the traits. `node scripts/compounder-screen.mjs` runs it as of today (`--asof 2022-04-01` for a point-in-time run) and writes `compounder/data/us/screen-latest.json` plus `reports/compounder-us-DATE.md`: the funnel, the quality list, the near-misses (four passes, one trait not computable), and a sensitivity table showing how many names each threshold set would yield. The weekly job `compounder-screen.yml` keeps the report current. Tests: `node scripts/test-compounder.mjs` (also part of `npm test`).

**Prices (US).** `scripts/compounder-prices-us.mjs` pulls daily bars for every screenable ticker plus the Russell 2000 from Yahoo Finance's chart endpoint (no key; Stooq turned out to need a login for bulk and to refuse scripts per symbol). Closes are split- and dividend-adjusted, and dollar turnover in $ millions is stored alongside, so `momentum.js`'s turnover floor means "$M" here. The store is `compounder/data/us/prices/YYYY.json` (plain JSON since 6 Nov 2026 — gzipped files made every daily commit a fresh 10 MB blob; older years may still be `.json.gz`, which readers accept), the same shape as `data/history/YYYY.json`, which is what lets `engine.buildBars()` and the momentum engine read it unchanged.

```
node scripts/compounder-prices-us.mjs --backfill                # six years for ~3,100 symbols, ~10 min
node scripts/compounder-prices-us.mjs                           # daily: last 10 sessions, appended
node scripts/compounder-prices-us.mjs --symbols QLYS,IESC,^RUT  # a few names, for checking
```

With prices present the screen changes in three ways: market cap becomes last close × cover-page shares instead of the 10-K public float; passers trading under $3M a day on average (20 bars) drop out of the quality list into their own section; and the momentum overlay runs over the quality list with the desk's own parameters (six-month return skipping the last month, within 20% of the 52-week high, Russell 2000 above its 200-day average as the gate). The report gains a **moving** column and a **Quality AND momentum** list, which is the only list meant to trigger an alert. The job `compounder-prices.yml` runs every weekday after the US close and re-runs the screen; the first time, run it from the Actions tab with the **backfill** box ticked.

**The backtest (US).** `scripts/compounder-backtest-us.mjs` (`npm run backtest:compounder`) replays the screen point-in-time on the first trading day of every quarter the store supports, ranks every priced company inside the cap band on the whole-universe composite (passers first per trait, then failers by value), and holds the top decile, the bottom decile, the quality list and the quality-and-momentum intersection for twelve months against the Russell 2000. It reports the pass criteria from `screen.json`, the survivor gap per cohort and cap bucket with each missing name tagged from the EDGAR store, a ±20% robustness table for every threshold, and two diagnostics (a value-only composite; every priced filer with no cap band). Options: `--from`/`--to`, `--deciles 5`, `--band all`, `--no-robustness`. First run, 5 Nov 2026: **fail** — top decile beat bottom decile in 57% of cohorts against a 70% bar, and every portfolio's lead over the index is explained by the store holding only today's survivors (`reports/compounder-backtest-us-2026-11-05.md`; the reasoning and what a second attempt would need are in `compounder/SCREEN.md`).

Attempt two (`--screen compounder/screen-v0.3.json`, profitability level as the rank) failed too: 50%. Not built, and parked with the shelving: the dashboard page, the early-detector reports, and India.

## Daily use

New to the page, or showing it to someone who is? Every section title has a small **?** beside it: hover, click or tap it for two short paragraphs — what the section is, and what a reader can do with it — in plain words (`help.js`).

The site is read top-down. The **regime** strip tells you whether to be trading at all (weak breadth halves every position size automatically). The **momentum portfolio** comes next because it is the only list with a positive record, over five years and over ten: the names to hold equal-weighted, the regime gate, and each name's trailing stop. The **sector board** shows where the money is; only the top three sectors with a score of 1 or more, two or more mapped stocks and a positive day or 30-day average count as leaders. Leadership adds 15 points to a setup's score and decides the two watch lists; it does not gate the setups — a breakout can fire in any sector and a pullback in any but the three lagging ones. The **swing setups** card has four tabs: Setups (watch only) — pullback buys and confirmed breakouts with the trigger level, the level below which the setup is void, the 2R level and a paper quantity; since 12 Nov 2026 pullbacks on names in the momentum engine's top 30 carry a green *momentum #rank* badge and sit first — the slice that passed on that day's names (+0.16R over 638 trades) and failed on the full membership (+0.08R over 587 trades; +0.09R over 594 before the weekend sessions were loaded), paper-traded under the journal setup "Momentum pullback" as a live check, nothing more; since 5 Nov 2026 these are **not orders**, because over five years the rules are breakeven after costs (+0.01R per trade on corporate-action-adjusted history; −0.08R before the adjustment), over ten years of point-in-time membership −0.05R over 872 trades, and no tested change reaches the +0.1R bar (see the backtest card and `reports/swing-variants-2026-11-05-adjusted.md`) — Set alerts (names within 2% of a 52-week high and the exact close to wait for), Watch (strong days in leading sectors, tagged "don't chase"), and Avoid (near 52-week lows or down 8%+ in 30 days). Each row carries a **score** (0–100): base 50, plus sector leadership, relative strength, moving-average trend, volume, RSI extension and candle confirmation; the backtest shows it has not separated winners from losers. Click a row for the full reasoning, the indicator readings and a **Log this trade** button that prefills the journal — the way to paper-trade the setups. Turning the queue back into orders is a one-word change (`swingMode: 'orders'` in `engine.js` DEFAULTS) that a backtest above +0.1R over 300+ trades has to earn first.

Pullback detection and relative volume need a few days of history; the tool gets sharper after its first week.

### Your data

Snapshots are committed to the repo, so every device sees the same market data. Your capital, risk settings, sector overrides and journal are stored in the browser you use (localStorage). Use **Backup** in the journal card to download them as a JSON file and **Restore** on another device. Do this before clearing browser data.

**Open positions — today's line.** Once the journal has an open trade, a card above it gives one line per position from fixed rules: hold, raise the stop to a stated price, or exit — with the date and level that triggered it. It applies what the backtest applies (stop, 2R target, breakeven after +1R, 15-bar time stop for swing setups; a 20% trailing stop and the rank-30 rule for a momentum name) and has no opinion. Close the trade in the journal when you act on an exit line; until then it stays at the top of the card.

**The Discretionary book.** Your own calls — something read in an annual report, a product you keep seeing — go in the journal under the setup *Discretionary*, so they are scored instead of argued about. The form holds the budget: two open positions at most, each at half the normal size, and one sentence saying why, written at entry. The Performance card reads the journal book by book in R; at the three- and six-month checks the Discretionary line is read beside the paper book and the index fund. It never changes a rule.

The Telegram digest sizes positions from `config.json`, not from the browser settings, so keep the two in step.

## Configuration

`config.json`:

| key | meaning |
|---|---|
| `index` | NSE index whose constituents to track: `NIFTY 500` (default), `NIFTY 50`, `NIFTY NEXT 50`, `NIFTY MIDCAP 100` … any name the NSE market-watch dropdown offers |
| `historyDays` | how many past snapshots the engine reads for pullback / volume logic |
| `keepDays` | snapshots older than this are deleted from `data/snapshots/` |
| `alertIntraday` | send a Telegram digest even when the snapshot is from before 2:30 pm |
| `reminders` | `[{date, text, repeat?, days?}]` — the digest lists each one under DESK CHECKS DUE on its date and for seven days after (or for `days` days, for a short note); `repeat` is `monthly`, `quarterly` or `semiannual`. Edit the entry when the check is done |
| `risk` | `capital`, `riskPct`, `minRR`, `maxPos` used in the digest's sizing |
| `paper` | `from`, `capital`, `benchmarks` — the paper book: the momentum engine's rules replayed from `from` on that capital, beside the listed indices (their closes are saved in `data/benchmarks/live.json`). `books` (21) — that many books, one started at the close of each session from `from`; `fund` — the index each is read against; `starts` — the books' start days, to be filled in once all have started so that a session loaded later cannot move them |
| `momentum` | optional; overrides for the momentum engine's parameters in the daily job, same names as `DEFAULTS` in `momentum.js` |
| `positions` | `[{sym, date, entry, stop, target?, qty?, setup}]` — open trades the digest should follow: each gets one rule line a day under OPEN POSITIONS (hold / raise stop / exit, the same line the site's card shows). Empty by default. The site's **Copy for the digest** button produces the block from the journal. This file is public: do not paste positions you would not publish |

`data/sectors.json` maps symbols to sectors for anything the built-in map in `engine.js` doesn't cover (Nifty 500 has many such names; they show as "Unmapped" and never qualify as a leading sector until mapped). The site's "Assign sector" box changes your browser only; copy the mapping into this file so the job agrees.

To change the schedule, edit `.github/workflows/daily.yml` (times are UTC; 12:30 UTC = 18:00 IST): the cron lines are the ladder of start slots, and the two minute-of-day numbers in the *Slot gate* step (195 = 03:15 UTC, 750 = 12:30 UTC) are the window in which a scheduled run does nothing. For an earlier digest lower the 750; the 16:00 table is final well before six.

## When the fetch fails

NSE sits behind a bot filter that occasionally refuses requests from cloud IP ranges. The job retries four times with fresh cookies and, if it still fails, sends you a Telegram alert instead of a digest: what NSE answered and on which host (`HTTP 403 from www.nseindia.com`), the last snapshot on file, and a link to the run's log. On a scheduled evening the later slots of the ladder try again on their own, and the digest comes from the first that gets through — so one alert followed by a digest needs nothing from you. Once the day's snapshot is on file the remaining slots stop without calling NSE at all (since 21 Nov 2026; before that each asked NSE again, and on 19 and 20 Nov one a night was refused and failed after the digest had already gone). A run that fails anywhere else — checkout, Node set-up, the commit — sends its own short alert, and so do the four other workflows. From some connections NSE now answers on `www.nseindia.in`; every script follows it to whichever host answers (`scripts/nse.mjs`). If no digest has come by morning, three fallbacks, in order of effort:

1. Re-run the workflow later from the Actions tab (blocks are often temporary).
2. Run `npm run fetch` on your own computer and `git push` the result — a home connection is almost never blocked.
3. Upload the CSV from the NSE page (Market Data → Equity/Stock → download) in the site's Manual snapshot panel. That stores it in your browser only, but the signals work.

**When the digest is late.** The job takes about half a minute; if the digest arrives hours after six, GitHub's scheduler started the job late (free-tier scheduled runs are queued behind everything else and can slip by hours at busy times). Measured in November 2026 the delay was 7–10 hours on this job every day, and 4–8 hours on the other workflows. Since 18 Nov 2026 `daily.yml` therefore has a slot every hour from 02:32 UTC and a gate: a scheduled run that starts before 12:30 UTC (18:00 IST) stops in seconds, the first one after it does the work, later ones find the day's snapshot on file and stop without calling NSE. The Actions list shows which slot won (each run prints a notice with its slot and start time). If the digest is still late after that — the delay has grown past ten hours, or the morning slots are not being started at all — trigger the job from an outside clock instead: create a fine-grained personal access token (Settings → Developer settings → Fine-grained tokens) limited to this repository with Actions: read and write, and set up a free job on cron-job.org (or any scheduler) that at 12:30 UTC Mon–Fri sends `POST https://api.github.com/repos/YOUR-USER/nifty-swing-desk/actions/workflows/daily.yml/dispatches` with headers `Authorization: Bearer <token>`, `Accept: application/vnd.github+json` and body `{"ref":"main"}`. That starts the job within a minute. The cron entries can stay as a backstop.

If blocks become the norm, register your own PC as a GitHub self-hosted runner and change `runs-on` in the workflow to `self-hosted`; the job then runs from your IP on the same schedule, as long as the machine is on.

NSE changes its website from time to time. The job calls `/api/NextApi/apiClient/marketWatchApi?functionName=getIndicesData` (the endpoint the live-equity page itself uses as of October 2026). If that moves, `scripts/fetch.mjs` is the only file to update: `E.rowsFromNseApi` in `engine.js` documents the field names it expects.

## Benchmark index

The history files carry **NIFTY 50** as the benchmark (relative strength, the 20-day index return, the momentum gate). When `config.index` is a different index, its market-watch payload only includes that index's own row, so the daily job fetches the NIFTY 50 row separately; the engine refuses to splice a differently named index close into the series (a NIFTY 500 close in a NIFTY 50 series looks like a 7% crash). If the NIFTY 50 fetch fails on a given day, that day's index bar is left empty and the gate uses the latest available bar that day. Since 21 Nov 2026 (audit A8) the hole does not stay: at the next run the job fills yesterday's close from today's own NIFTY 50 row (its previous close) and asks NSE's index history for any older one, writes the bar into the history file and says `INDEX REPAIRED` in the digest. Until a bar is repaired the gate's 200-day average is taken over the last 200 closes on file, stepping over up to five missing ones — one missing close moves it by under a tenth of a percent of the level (0.003% when tried on November 2026's data), nothing accumulates, and the digest carries an `INDEX GAP` line for as long as it lasts. Only with more than five closes missing does the gate fall back to breadth (more than half the stocks above their own 200-day), and then the digest says `GATE FROM BREADTH`. Before this change a single missing bar sent the gate to breadth for the next 200 sessions without a word, which is what happened from 26 Oct to 5 Nov 2026. If a hole will not heal — NSE refusing the index-history call from GitHub — fill it from home: BACKFILL.md, "A missing index bar".

## The rules, in one paragraph

Regime: breadth ≥ 60% advancing with the index up is Strong; ≤ 40% or index below −0.5% is Weak (half size); otherwise Mixed. Sector score = today's average move + 30-day average ÷ 6 + 2 × (breadth − 0.5) + average relative strength ÷ 4; the top three with score ≥ 1, at least two mapped stocks and a positive day or 30-day average lead. Relative strength = 20-day return minus the Nifty's, ranked as a percentile. **52W breakout**: within 2% of the 52-week high, 30-day change ≥ 0, today not down more than 1.5%; entry 0.3% above the *prior* high (the 52-week high before today — NSE's figure includes today's bar), stop 2 × ATR(14) below entry (floor 2%), target at the configured reward:risk; actionable only on a close above the level and not more than 4% through it. **Pullback buy**: an impulse day of ≥ 3% within the last five sessions, today between −3% and +1%, holding above the impulse low and not more than 2% under the 20-day average, 30-day change ≥ 5%, not in a lagging sector; entry 0.3% above today's high, stop half an ATR under the lower of today's low and the impulse low. **Momentum watch**: up ≥ 2% today in a leading sector with 30-day ≥ 5% — never bought on the day. **Sector leader watch**: in a leading sector with 30-day ≥ 5% and today above −3%; no trigger. **Avoid**: within 5% of the 52-week low, down ≥ 8% in 30 days, or below both moving averages with a negative 30-day change. **Score**: 50 base; leading sector +15 / lagging −10; relative strength top 30% +10 / bottom 30% −10; price > 20-day > 50-day +10 / below both −10; volume ≥ 1.5× average +10; RSI ≥ 75 −15; bullish candle +5 / bearish −10; an Act-now setup under 50 becomes an alert. Quantity = (capital × risk%) ÷ (entry − stop), capped at `maxPos`% of capital. On a snapshot from before 2:30 pm a breakout is an alert, not an order (a pullback is not downgraded; the job runs after the close, so this matters only for a manual midday snapshot); max four open positions, never two in one sector, stop to breakeven at +1R.

Run the backtest before trading it, then paper-trade the first weeks and judge the system on expectancy (average R per closed trade), which the Performance card tracks for you.

## Maybe, once everything else is built: an India compounder

Not scheduled and not promised (owner, 19 Nov 2026). It is looked at only after the audit items are closed and the US compounder has been built out — and only if a US screen has passed its test, because until then there is no screen to port. This section is the list of what it would take, so the question does not have to be researched twice. Background and the vendor comparison are under the two Compounder items in "Where things stand".

**What is already in the repository.** The results-dates pulls of 13 and 19 Nov 2026 index NSE's own filings: every row links to the filing's XBRL file and carries its broadcast time — 80,507 filings from May 2018 to March 2025 and 31,503 since, on about 2,400 symbols (`data/results/raw/`); 875 of the 895 names that were ever in the NIFTY 500 have at least one. They are as originally filed, time-stamped, and include companies that have since left the market: what EDGAR is to the US screen. Point-in-time NIFTY 500 membership exists too (`data/membership/members.json`).

**What it needs, in order.**

1. **A look inside a dozen XBRL files before anything else** (home run; NSE's archive refuses cloud connections). Quarterly results carry the profit-and-loss account every quarter, the balance sheet only half-yearly and the cash-flow statement half-yearly from 2019. Whether NSE's files hold those two is not verified, and ROCE, leverage and cash conversion cannot be computed without them. If they are missing, stop here: the free route does not work and the choice is a paid vendor or no India screen.
2. **A downloader and an extractor for the filings** — the counterpart of `compounder-fetch-us.mjs`: every filing's file saved raw, then annual figures per company with the date each became public, standalone and consolidated kept apart, restated comparatives never overwriting what was first filed.
3. **Prices for every listed name, delisted included**: `node scripts/backfill.mjs --universe all` from home (each day's bhavcopy holds that day's listed stocks, so the store is survivor-complete by construction), then `corporate-actions` on the wider universe. Fix A17 (NSE's host) and A18 (weekend sessions) first.
4. **Shares outstanding for market caps** — paid-up capital ÷ face value from the same filings, checked against a handful of quote pages as the US caps were.
5. **A benchmark with dividends and a liquidity floor in rupees**: NIFTY 500 TR or NIFTY Smallcap 250 TR from niftyindices.com, loaded the way the momentum benchmarks were.
6. **The hypothesis, the thresholds and the pass mark written and committed before the first run**, as for every test here. The US thresholds are not carried over unread: Indian accounts differ (promoter holdings, Ind-AS from 2016–17, banks and NBFCs filed on their own formats and screened out or handled apart).
7. **The engine**: `compounder.js` was written against the EDGAR store, and how much of it is tied to that shape has not been checked; at the least the extractor's field map, the currency and the calendar (fiscal years ending in March) change.

**The limit that access does not fix: depth.** Filings start in 2018, with FY2017 as a comparative. A company needs six fiscal years before it can be ranked, so the first cohort is mid-2022, and a backtest today would have about a dozen quarterly cohorts where the US test had 44 — too few for the 70% test as written. Two ways through, both with a cost: buy FY2010–2017 from a vendor (EODHD is the one to test first, on its free tier; the figures would be as-restated and the delisted companies missing, so every result would carry a look-ahead and survivorship caveat), or start the screen as a paper record on the filings that exist and judge it when there are enough cohorts.

**What it would cost.** Nothing in subscriptions on the free route; at a guess two or three sessions and three home runs (the XBRL files, the all-names price load, corporate actions). The repository would grow by the size of the filings store and roughly four times the price history, which is the argument for keeping that store, like Sharadar's, in a second private repository.
